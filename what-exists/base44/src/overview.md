# sculptura

a platform for creators who design in cad and want their work to exist as real physical objects.

---

## what it is

sculptura connects designers who work in 3d modeling software with a manufacturing pipeline that turns those designs into metal objects. it is not a marketplace for mass-produced goods. every artifact is made to order, from a design that a specific person made.

creators publish their designs. buyers request them. the platform routes each order to a manufacturing partner. the creator earns a markup on every piece that ships.

sculptura also includes a parametric design studio where creators can build and iterate on jewelry designs directly in the browser, using a layer-based system that controls geometry, surface decoration, stone settings, and material - all visualized in real time with three.js.

---

## who it is for

primarily: independent designers, jewelers, and makers who already work in tools like rhino, fusion 360, or blender, and who want to sell the output of that work without building an ecommerce site, managing inventory, or dealing with production logistics.

secondarily: buyers who want objects that feel genuinely made rather than manufactured at scale. people who care about the design thinking behind an object, not just the object itself.

---

## what problem it solves

cad designers often finish a model and have no clear path to turning it into something physical and sellable. the options are: commission a prototype yourself, find a manufacturer and manage the relationship, or give up on the idea.

sculptura handles the production side entirely. the designer uploads a file, sets their earnings, and gets paid when someone orders. the complexity of manufacturing, quality checks, and shipping is not their problem.

the design studio solves a different problem: some creators want to start from a parametric base rather than from scratch. the studio lets them configure jewelry geometry interactively, then export it as openscad code or send it directly to a print service.

---

## how it feels to use

the interface is intentionally quiet. no noise, no gamification, no aggressive calls to action. it is designed to feel like a well-made publication: clean type, soft backgrounds, careful spacing.

the market account system is built around the assumption that creators are serious about their work but do not want the overhead of managing accounts. a single key is all they need. the review process is slow and deliberate because the platform is curated by default.

the design studio is precise and minimal. each step of the build wizard focuses on one decision. the 3d viewport updates in real time. the tools feel like tools, not toys.
