# piercing placements: the anatomy knowledge base

this is the research the piercing hold needs before anything ships: a
placement-by-placement map of ear anatomy, the jewelry types each
placement takes, and the dimensional standards paracraft must
understand to even model the right object. this file is knowledge, not
a release switch: the [hold](README.md) on piercing-adjacent claims
stands, and nothing here approves a piercing-safe offer.

## why paracraft needs this

"earring" is not one object. a helix piece is often a small flat-back
stud or a snug hoop around the rim; a conch piece is a large stud
inside the bowl or a thick-diameter hoop around it; a daith is a
curved piece threading the innermost fold; an industrial is one long
bar through two holes. if the studio only understands "stud, hoop,
drop", it will generate geometry that is wrong for the anatomy the
buyer named. placements are therefore a first-class vocabulary axis in
the studio: choosing "helix" changes wearable depth, diameter range,
curvature, and mass constraints, not just a label.

## the placement map (candidate, from retail piercing guides)

dimensions are retail-reported typical ranges, **not** partner-verified
specs; every row needs specialist evidence before any becomes a
constraint set.

| placement | typical jewelry | gauge (retail-typical) | size ranges (retail-typical) |
|---|---|---|---|
| lobe (standard/upper) | stud, flat-back labret, hoop, huggie | 20g (0.8mm) or 18g (1.0mm) | post length 6-10mm; hoop 6-12mm |
| helix (rim cartilage) | flat-back labret, small hoop, clicker | 16g (1.2mm) typical | 5-8mm wearable/hoop diameter |
| forward helix | flat-back labret, small hoop | 16g | small diameters, front-facing ends |
| tragus / anti-tragus | flat-back labret, tiny hoop | 16g | 5-6mm |
| conch (inner/outer bowl) | large flat-back stud, thick-diameter hoop | 16g | 6-12mm |
| daith (innermost fold) | curved bar, clicker, seamless hoop | 16g (14g seen) | ~8mm curvature to clear the fold |
| rook (inner ridge) | small curved bar, small hoop | 16g | 5.5-7mm |
| snug (inner ridge, horizontal) | small curved bar | 16g | anatomy-dependent |
| industrial (scaffold, two holes) | single long straight bar | 14g (1.6mm) seen, 16g | bar length anatomy-paired, 32-40mm retail-typical |
| orbital (two rim holes, one hoop) | hoop spanning both | 16g | diameter spans both holes |

gauge chart anchor: 20g = 0.8mm, 18g = 1.0mm, 16g = 1.2mm, 14g =
1.6mm wire/post thickness.

## what this changes in the studio

- placement is a studio parameter axis alongside family: it narrows
  the object type (labret vs hoop vs curved bar), gauge, wearable
  length, curvature, and mass budget.
- the attachment interface ([findings](../findings/README.md)) is
  placement-aware: a flat back for a helix is a different spec than a
  butterfly catch for a lobe; thread-through pieces (daith clicker)
  may have no separate finding at all.
- paracraft constraint sets per placement (candidate above) must be
  evidence-backed before any placement ships: gauge tolerances, wear
  comfort, finish at contact, retention under hair/glasses/contact.
- standard lobe earrings remain the default lane; placements beyond
  the lobe are candidate vocabulary, subject to the hold.

## expanded candidate map: facial and body placements (2026-09-29)

owner-supplied reference table extends the map past the ear-only set
above. same rule applies without exception: styling-guide content,
**not** gauge or medical specification; every row still needs
specialist evidence before it becomes a paracraft constraint, and
nothing here approves any placement for sale. this pass only sorts
placements into rough studio-relevance bands so future research effort
goes where the product actually needs it.

### more ear placements (beyond the original ten)

| placement | where it goes | typical jewelry |
|---|---|---|
| flat | flat upper cartilage area | small studs, stars, crystals, constellation clusters |
| vertical helix | vertically through the upper outer rim | curved barbell or stacked small rings |
| transverse lobe | horizontally through the lower lobe | curved barbell or decorative connecting bar |
| hidden helix | cartilage positioned so jewelry tucks into the ear | tiny curved barbell, chain, "ear climber" style |

these four slot directly into the existing ear table's format and
mechanics (flat-back studs, curved barbells, small hoops): no new
jewelry *type* is introduced, only new placements for types paracraft
already needs to model.

### facial placements (new category for this map)

| placement | where it goes | typical jewelry |
|---|---|---|
| bridge | horizontal, upper nose between the eyes | straight barbell, small balls or gems |
| high nostril | higher than a standard nostril piercing | tiny flat-back studs, often paired |
| rhino | vertical, through the tip of the nose | curved barbell, ends visible above and below |
| septum | soft tissue beneath the nasal cartilage | horseshoe barbell, clicker ring, seamless hoop |
| vertical labret | vertical through the lower lip | beads above and below the lip |
| ashley | through the lower lip, gem visible on the lip | single decorative gem |
| jestrum / medusa | vertical or centered above the upper lip | curved barbell or flat-back |
| dahlia | paired, near the corners of the mouth | matching studs |
| dimple / cheek | through the cheek, at the dimple position | flat-back studs |
| smiley | through the upper lip frenulum (inside) | small circular ring or horseshoe |
| tongue web | through the thin tissue under the tongue | small curved barbell |

facial placements introduce jewelry types the ear table does not
cover (horseshoe barbells, clickers) as first-class candidates; they
already appear in the studio-wide jewelry-style list below.

### body placements (new category, mixed relevance)

| placement | where it goes | typical jewelry |
|---|---|---|
| navel variations | inverted, floating, double, upper/lower navel | curved barbell, often with dangling charms, opals, crystals |
| surface piercing | a bar under flat skin (collarbone, nape, chest, hips) | surface bar, two small visible ends |
| dermal anchor | single point under the skin (cheekbone, collarbone, chest, lower back, hip) | one gem, disk, spike, or tiny charm on the skin |
| hand or finger dermal | dermal on the hand or finger | small permanent-looking gemstone |
| nipple |: | straight barbell, sometimes ornate ends or chains |
| christina, fourchette, triangle, guiche | genital placements | curved barbells, rings; highly anatomy-dependent, specialist-only |

### studio-wide jewelry style vocabulary (from this pass)

types that recur across placements above and belong in the studio's
jewelry-type vocabulary regardless of which specific placement ships
first: flat-back labrets, curved barbells, circular/horseshoe
barbells, clickers (hinged rings), seamless hoops, chains connecting
two piercings, threadless tops (interchangeable decorative ends), and
tunnels/plugs for stretched lobes. tunnels/plugs are their own
material and sizing problem (glass, titanium, wood, silicone) distinct
from precious-metal casting and are flagged here only as a vocabulary
note, not a candidate for the metal-only casting pipeline.

## scope bands (owner review needed, not a decision)

sorting the full list by how directly it matters to a metal-only
jewelry marketplace, so research effort is prioritized rather than
flat:

- **band 1, clearly in future scope:** all ear placements (original
  ten plus the four above) and most facial placements (bridge, high
  nostril, rhino, septum, vertical labret, ashley, jestrum/medusa,
  dahlia). mainstream retail jewelry categories, metal findings map
  cleanly.
- **band 2, plausible but needs its own call:** dimple/cheek, smiley,
  tongue web (specialist/oral, lower retail volume), navel
  variations, surface piercing, dermal anchor, hand/finger dermal,
  nipple. these are real retail jewelry categories (dermal jewelry
  and nipple barbells are sold at mainstream piercing/jewelry
  retailers) but carry more anatomy-specific risk and are a distinct
  product-scope decision, not an automatic yes.
- **band 3, flagged for explicit owner sign-off before any research
  spend:** christina, fourchette, triangle, guiche (genital
  placements). real piercing-industry categories, but they raise
  support, moderation, and brand-positioning questions well beyond
  jewelry geometry; nothing here recommends including or excluding
  them, only that they should not enter research quietly alongside
  the rest.

## scope decision (owner, 2026-09-29)

bands 2 and 3 are **set off indefinitely**: no research spend, no
vocabulary work, no paracraft constraint development for any of them.
they stay in this map as recorded knowledge only. band 1 (all ear
placements plus mainstream facial) remains the only active future-scope
candidate set, still under the research hold.
