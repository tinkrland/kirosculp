# colloquial fit copy: draft per-size lines

draft buyer-facing copy for the colloquial fit layer, in the register of:
"us size 2 - usually not suitable for adult pinkies either, but if slender
fingers may fit on first knuckle".

companion to [sizing reality](sizing-reality.md); every line below is
grounded in that file's verified anchors (women's ring finger 6-6.5, women's
pinky 3-5 with 4 most common, men's ring finger 10 range 8-12, men's pinky
6-8 with 7 average, midi worn half to one full size down, toe rings 3-4).
status: draft copy, not ruled. mm stays the ground truth; these lines render
from it.

## ring size lines (us whole sizes 2-13)

- us 2 - usually not suitable for adult pinkies either, but if slender
  fingers may fit on first knuckle. otherwise think child size or toe ring
- us 3 - the smallest size most adult hands have anywhere; a slim women's
  pinky, or a midi placement on an average hand
- us 4 - the most common women's pinky size; on very petite hands a slim
  ring finger
- us 5 - a pinky on wider hands, a slim ring finger, and a very common
  dainty-band or promise size
- us 6 - the average women's ring finger; the size most women call "just a
  normal ring"
- us 6.5 - the most common engagement size; when guessing for a gift, this
  is the single most likely fit for a woman's ring finger
- us 7 - upper end of women's ring fingers; also a go-to statement or
  middle-finger size on smaller hands
- us 8 - around the women's thumb spot, or the start of the men's ring
  finger range on slimmer hands
- us 9 - a typical women's thumb, an upper men's pinky, or a slim men's
  ring finger
- us 10 - the average men's ring finger; the men's equivalent of the
  women's 6
- us 11 - above-average men's ring finger, or a men's thumb on smaller
  hands
- us 12 - upper men's range; a common men's thumb size
- us 13 - the top of the common us chart; large hands and thumbs

## how to read the lines (schema-facing notes)

- each line renders from mm ground truth, never the reverse. the copy is a
  view keyed by size band, not data
- the "thumb" lines are the least evidenced: research gives ring-finger and
  pinky anchors directly, thumb ranges are inferred from the usual
  two-to-three-sizes-up-from-ring-finger rule. flag before treating thumb
  copy as more than helpful hint language
- half sizes read the same way: "us 6.5 - the most common engagement size"
  exists because 6.5 is the recorded mode, not because half steps are
  everywhere. iso/jp/in buyers see their own system's nearest whole label
  instead (their systems have no half sizes)
- the lines deliberately say what a size is *for*, not just what it
  measures: "promise size", "engagement size", "midi placement" are the
  occasion words buyers actually use, the same vocabulary as the ring-type
  table in [sizes beyond rings](../sizes-beyond-rings.md)
- copy must stay honest under edge cases: slender hands, larger hands and
  men's fingers all appear in the lines, so no buyer reads a size chart that
  silently assumes one body

## the pattern generalizes to other types

the same one-line register works wherever a measurement needs translating:

- bangle, in market - "2.6 - the most common women's size in india, but
  measure your hand over the knuckles, not your wrist"
- bangle, cn jade practice - "56mm - measure across your knuckles and divide
  by pi; this is a hand measurement, not a wrist one"
- bracelet, us - "7in - fits the average women's wrist with the standard
  half-inch of ease"
- necklace - "45cm - sits at the collarbone on most women; the single most
  common length outside the us"
- hoop - "8mm inner diameter - a huggie; hugs the lobe rather than hanging"

same rule as rings: the number is ground truth, the line is the view.
