# ideal sculptura buyers: profiles

hypothesis profiles built from oct 2026 external research. each profile lists
what they know, what confuses them, and what the size/occasion research says
sculptura must handle for them. nothing here is a ruled product decision.

## profile 1: the self-purchaser (primary)

the dominant modern fine-jewelry buyer. buys for themselves, for daily wear,
stacking, and self-expression rather than milestones.

- evidence: 80% of us adults buy fine jewelry for themselves (briteco);
  self-purchase trend grew 58% between 2021 and 2024 (business of fashion
  state of fashion 2026); most appealing price point around $500, average
  self-purchase spend around $1,200; millennials lead at 86%
- demographic tilt: millennials 45% and gen z 35% of custom jewelry buyers
  (2025), gen z prefers sub-$250 pieces
- what she knows: her own sizes (roughly), her aesthetic, her stack
- what confuses her: cross-market size systems when shopping a global
  marketplace; whether a paracraft param change moves the fit; band-width
  fit effects
- what sculptura must handle: mm-first sizing with local-system display;
  fit descriptors for stacking/midi/pinky contexts; taste-based discovery
  (already the affinity direction); no skin/body-feature inference from
  metal preference (ruled elsewhere)

## profile 2: the proposal/gift buyer (highest size risk)

buys for someone else, often deliberately without asking, often does not
know the recipient's size at all. the occasion compresses the decision:
one ring, one moment, no retry.

- evidence: nearly half of men admit guessing engagement ring size
  (jewelers of america 2023); 1 in 3 couples resize before the first photo
  shoot; documented folk practices (tracing rings, sizers during sleep,
  friend recruitment, buy-large strategy)
- knows: the occasion, maybe her style taste (or thinks he does)
- does not know: her size, sometimes her preference; may not know his own
- what sculptura must handle: a discreet sizing path; resize-friendliness
  surfaced as a material fact (gold/platinum yes, tungsten/titanium no);
  colloquial fit anchors ("average women's ring finger is us 6-6.5") so
  at least the guess lands in the right county; post-proposal resize or
  recompile path, which parametric production could make cheap and fast
  relative to traditional retail
- gift-buyer edge cases: anniversary/mother's day/promise rings; the same
  blind-size problem at lower stakes

## profile 3: the commission buyer

wants a specific piece that does not exist yet, or a modification of
something seen. engages with the artist, tolerates lead time, cares about
the process.

- evidence: commission pain points are process pains: many revision
  iterations, communication overhead, opaque pricing (work orders with
  design specs, materials, labor, deadlines), unpredictable lead times
  (rapaport, jewelsteps)
- knows: what they want, roughly. often not what it costs or how long it
  takes
- what sculptura must handle: structured commission intake that captures
  occasion, finger(s) and size source, budget ceiling, deadline; pricing
  transparency (public hourly rates are the creator's own, sculptura never
  recommends prices, ruled elsewhere); iteration boundaries

## profile 4: the collector / connoisseur

repeat buyer, often follows specific creators, buys across storefronts,
values provenance and hallmarking (vienna convention/ccm material lives on
the security/paracraft legs). smallest volume, highest ticket. needs
authenticity signals and assay/hallmark facts, not size education.

## cross-cutting buyer rules from the research

- sizing is the top controllable return driver: one deployment cut ring
  returns 17.2% -> 6.1% with proper sizing capture
- multiple-size deliberate orders: a real buyer behavior, budget-able via
  escrow/refund rules rather than fighting it
- size display should be market-localized (us numeric, uk letters, eu mm,
  jp numeric, in local) with mm always shown; checkout sees destination
  market, never buyer-declared origin (consistent with the ruled routing
  model)
