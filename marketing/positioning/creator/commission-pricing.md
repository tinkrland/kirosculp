# commission pricing belongs to the artist

flat, hourly and milestone commission terms are creator-authored. sculptura
supports recording and enforcing those terms; it does not recommend rates,
price tiers or a supposedly correct value for the artist's labor.

## no ai commission-price adviser

when someone asks what to charge, point them toward other creatives in
creative forums or subreddits. do not provide an ai bot that sets, scores or
suggests their commission rate. tessa is a studio design tool, not a pricing
manager, and the platform must not introduce that role under another name.

this also rules out ai hints to increase rates because buyers are american,
ai comparisons presented as recommended prices, and country-based wage
baselines. an artist may make their own market choices without the platform
turning those choices into its valuation of their worth.

## internal time value and public rate are different

future creator deep-dive reporting may compare time spent actively iterating
with tessa against earnings. the creator can privately choose an internal
hourly value for their own calculations.

that value is not a public commission rate, a minimum acceptable offer, or
a recommendation. a private $8/hour time value and a public $68/hour or
€90/hour commission rate can coexist. the commissioner is not entitled to
the internal number or a geographic explanation of the difference.

reporting can show earnings, recorded time and earnings after the creator's
chosen time valuation. it must not turn those facts into "you are undercharging"
or "your correct rate is x". the creator interprets the outcome, with peer
advice if wanted. time measurement and attribution still need implementation
design; these are future reporting directions, not existing features.

for designs sold repeatedly as ordinary listings, upfront design time must
not be deducted in full anew for every sale. that separate reporting question
does not convert listings into hourly commissions.

## per-sale protection is not a valuation of labor

the pricing requirement is to block a sale whose known applicable deductions
would leave creator net earnings negative, in either ordinary listing pricing
mode and with subscriber fee discounts accounted for. that arithmetic is not
an ai judgment about what the creator should earn per hour.

positive per-sale net does not itself guarantee recovery of creative time or
protection against every later refund, chargeback or cost overrun. those
allocation rules need explicit contracts, not a marketing promise that every
possible outcome has already been covered.
