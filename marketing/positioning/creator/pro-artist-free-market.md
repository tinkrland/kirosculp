# pro-artist does not mean anti-profit

sculptura is commercially ambitious. artists are allowed to be commercially
ambitious too. a profitable platform and well-paid creators are not opposing
objectives by definition.

## what we sell, and what we refuse to sell

sculptura's value is useful infrastructure: the studio, validated design
releases, selling tools, commission workflows and manufacturing orchestration.
software subscriptions and platform charges can fund that infrastructure.
pricing and fees still need sustainable economics; artist-first does not mean
unlimited free compute or a promise that every transaction has no costs.

what we do not sell is a shortcut to finding people who will accept less
because their country is perceived as poorer or their circumstances are
perceived as desperate. for commissions, sculptura intentionally withholds
creator geography and the soft signals that would hand it back to the buyer.
see [availability](availability.md).

## free market, without a geographic discount imposed on the artist

an artist in bangladesh or ghana may set a commission rate of $68/hour, or
an artist in bangladesh may choose €90/hour. these are examples of autonomy,
not platform-recommended rates or claims about typical earnings.

there is no requirement to justify that price against local wages, living
costs or what a western buyer thinks someone in that country should earn.
the rate belongs to the artist. the commissioner can accept the work and
terms, decline them, or discuss scope within the artist's boundaries.

pro-artist does not guarantee demand or abolish a buyer's choice. it means
sculptura does not use nationality, economic vulnerability or an ai valuation
to pressure the creator toward cheaper labor. an offer failing to sell is
not proof that the creator was presumptuous for naming their price.

## not paternalism in another direction

we do not replace a buyer's low-wage assumption with a platform's western
wage baseline. a creator privately valuing their time at $8/hour is not
someone for sculptura to shame or automatically flag as undercharging.
the same creator can choose a much higher public commission rate.

[private profitability reporting](commission-pricing.md) can show outcomes.
it cannot determine what the artist ought to charge. asking other creatives
for advice is different from treating a platform bot as an authority on value.

## language to keep and language to avoid

use: "your commission rates are yours", "choose work and terms", and
"we earn by providing useful infrastructure".

avoid: "affordable talent from low-cost countries", "charge what is normal
where you live", "our ai knows your ideal rate", and "we generously allow
you to charge more than local wages". permission to value your own work is
not a gift from sculptura.

this is our commission positioning, not a factual claim that every other
jewelry business or freelance marketplace behaves the same way.
